Note on certainty: the 2026 circular confirms a written exam and a viva stage, but does not publish the interview format or weighting. Everything below is prepared from the standard structure of Bangladeshi private-bank TCO/Officer interviews. Treat it as practice, not as a leaked paper.

How a TCO viva actually works (typical): 3–5 member board, 5–10 minutes, mostly in Bangla with some English. They are checking three things — are you honest, are you accurate, and will you actually go to a rural branch without complaining. Answer briefly. Long answers create openings for harder questions.

A. The five questions that decide the interview

1. "Tell us about yourself."

"I am [name], from [district]. I completed my four-year bachelor's in [subject] from [university] with a CGPA of [x]. I am comfortable with numbers and with people — during my studies I [one concrete thing: tutoring, a part-time job, a volunteer programme]. I am applying to DBBL because it is the bank that took technology to rural Bangladesh, and the TCO role is where that actually happens, at the counter. I am willing to work in any branch you assign."

Why this works: it is 45 seconds, it contains one concrete fact, it mentions willingness to relocate, and it does not recite your CV.

2. "Why Dutch-Bangla Bank, and not another bank?"

"Three reasons. DBBL was the first local bank to bring ATM service to Bangladesh, in 2004, and it still runs the largest ATM network. Its mobile financial service, Rocket, was launched in 2011 and reached people who had never entered a branch. And its scholarship and cleft-lip programmes show it treats social responsibility as part of banking, not as advertising. I want to work in an institution where technology and inclusion are the actual business model."

3. "Why TCO and not MTO or Assistant Officer?"

"Because I want to start where the bank meets the customer. A cash officer sees every transaction that a policy document only describes — deposits, withdrawals, remittances, fraud attempts, elderly customers who cannot read a form. I would rather learn the bank from the counter first. I understand the TCO role is a one-year probation at Tk 26,000 and confirms at Tk 41,410, and I am applying with that clear in my mind."

Do not say: "TCO is easier" or "I did not qualify for MTO". Say you chose it.

4. "Are you willing to work in a rural branch, far from Dhaka?"

"Yes. I applied knowing the posting can be anywhere in Bangladesh. My family is supportive and I have lived outside Dhaka before. If I am posted in a sub-branch, I will treat it as the place where the job actually matters most — that is where a large share of DBBL's customers are."

Say yes without conditions. Any hesitation here ends the interview.

5. "What does a cash officer do?"

"A cash officer receives deposits and pays withdrawals, verifies signatures and instruments, counts and reconciles the cash drawer, and balances the day's transactions before closing. The job also includes detecting counterfeit notes, following KYC and AML rules, and explaining products to customers who may not be familiar with them. Accuracy is the core of it — a single miscount becomes a loss that has to be made good."

B. Banking knowledge questions

Question Model answer
What is CRR? Cash Reserve Ratio — the share of deposits banks must keep with Bangladesh Bank as cash; currently 4%, and it earns no interest.
What is SLR? Statutory Liquidity Ratio — the share that must be held in liquid assets such as cash, gold and government securities; 13% for conventional banks, 5.5% for Islamic banks.
What is the repo rate? The rate at which Bangladesh Bank lends short-term funds to commercial banks against securities; currently 9.50%.
Difference between repo and reverse repo? Repo: BB lends to banks (injects liquidity). Reverse repo: BB borrows from banks (absorbs liquidity).
What is the call money rate? The overnight interbank lending rate — the price at which banks lend their surplus to each other.
What is NPL? A Non-Performing Loan — a loan where interest or principal has remained overdue beyond the prescribed period; it stops generating income for the bank.
What is CAMELS? A bank rating framework: Capital adequacy, Asset quality, Management, Earnings, Liquidity, Sensitivity to market risk.
What is KYC? Know Your Customer — identifying a customer and understanding their transaction profile before and during the relationship, to prevent money laundering and fraud.
What is RTGS? Real Time Gross Settlement — high-value interbank transfers settled instantly and individually.
What is BEFTN? Bangladesh Electronic Funds Transfer Network — batch-based, low-value electronic transfers.
What is the difference between a debit card and a credit card? Debit spends money already in your account; credit spends the bank's money and must be repaid.
What is a Nostro account? Our bank's account held with a foreign bank, used for foreign currency transactions.
What is hypothecation? A charge on movable assets (stock, machinery) without transferring possession to the bank.
What is a letter of credit? A bank's undertaking to pay a seller on the buyer's behalf once specified documents are presented — the backbone of import/export trade.

If you do not know a term: "I am not certain of the exact definition, but my understanding is…" Honesty scores better than a confident wrong answer, especially in a bank.

C. Situational / integrity questions

"You are short by Tk 5,000 at the end of the day. What do you do?"

"First, I recount the drawer and recheck the day's vouchers, because most differences are a counting or posting error. If it is still short, I report it to the branch manager immediately — the same day, not the next morning — and write it in the cash difference register. I do not try to cover it from my own pocket quietly or adjust another entry, because that turns a small error into a records offence. If the difference is mine, I make it good according to the bank's rules."

"A customer offers you Tk 500 to process his withdrawal out of turn."

"I refuse politely and explain that the queue applies to everyone, and that accepting money would put both his account and my job at risk. If he is in genuine difficulty — an emergency, an elderly person, a medical case — I would ask the manager whether the branch can prioritise him through the proper channel."

"A customer wants to withdraw Tk 5 lakh in cash and appears nervous. What do you notice?"

"Large cash withdrawal, unusual nervousness, and possibly a third person directing him are the classic signs of a coercion or fraud case. I would verify his identity carefully, ask a simple question about the account to confirm he controls it, and if anything feels wrong, inform the manager before processing. Under the AML rules, a suspicious transaction must be reported, not just refused."

"You find a counterfeit note in a deposit."

"I stop and do not return the note to the customer. I inform the manager, and the note is impounded and reported according to Bangladesh Bank procedure, with a receipt given to the customer. I do not put it back into circulation or exchange it quietly."

"Your colleague asks you to sign a voucher you did not verify."

"I decline. I would only sign for something I have personally checked, and I would tell him why — it protects him as much as me. If he insists, I raise it with the manager."

"A relative asks you to check a customer's balance."

"I cannot. Account information is confidential, and disclosing it would breach both bank policy and the law. I would explain that even for family, the answer is no — and that the same rule protects their own account."

D. Customer-service questions

"An illiterate customer cannot fill in the deposit slip. What do you do?"

"I fill it in for him at the counter, read every field back to him, and ask him to confirm the amount and account number before I accept the cash. I never sign on his behalf. If the branch is busy, I ask a colleague to continue the queue while I finish properly — speed is not worth a wrong account number."

"A customer is angry because his cheque was returned."

"I let him finish, then explain the specific reason — insufficient funds, a signature mismatch, a post-dated cheque — in plain language, without blaming him. I tell him exactly what he needs to do next. Anger usually comes from not understanding why; once the reason is clear, most customers calm down."

"A customer insists on a service the bank does not offer."

"I explain clearly what we can do and offer the closest alternative, for example a different account type or a digital channel. If it is genuinely outside my authority, I refer him to the manager rather than promising something I cannot deliver."

E. Bangla — questions likely to be asked in Bangla

  • নিজের সম্পর্কে বলুন। — ৪৫ সেকেন্ডে: নাম, জেলা, শিক্ষা, একটি নির্দিষ্ট অভিজ্ঞতা, এবং যে কোনো শাখায় কাজ করার ইচ্ছা।
  • কেন ব্যাংকিং? — "সংখ্যার সাথে কাজ করতে ভালো লাগে, আর মানুষের সাথে সরাসরি যোগাযোগের সুযোগ আছে। ব্যাংকিং দুটোই দেয়।"
  • ক্যাশ অফিসারের কাজ কী? — আমানত গ্রহণ, উত্তোলন প্রদান, স্বাক্ষর যাচাই, নগদ হিসাব মিলানো, জাল নোট শনাক্তকরণ, KYC মেনে চলা।
  • গ্রামে পোস্টিং হলে সমস্যা আছে? — "না, আমি আবেদন করার সময়ই জেনেছি যে সারা দেশে পোস্টিং হতে পারে।"
  • ডাচ-বাংলা ব্যাংক কেন? — ১৯৯৬ সালে যাত্রা, প্রথম জয়েন্ট ভেঞ্চার ব্যাংক, ২০০৪ সালে প্রথম এটিএম, রকেট ২০১১।
  • আপনার শক্তি ও দুর্বলতা? — শক্তি: নিয়ম মেনে কাজ করা, হিসাব ঠিক রাখা। দুর্বলতা: নতুন পরিবেশে প্রথমে একটু সময় নিই — সমাধান হিসেবে আগে শিখে নিই।

F. Questions to ask them (ask one, not five)

  • "What does the first three months of training look like for a TCO — is it classroom, branch, or both?"
  • "In a sub-branch, how much of the role is cash handling versus customer service and account opening?"

Do not ask about salary, leave, or transfer policy in the first interview. Those are for HR after selection.

G. Ten-point viva checklist

  1. Wear formal, ironed clothes. Clean shoes. No strong perfume.
  2. Carry the admit card, NID, and one set of photocopies in a plain folder.
  3. Enter, greet, sit only when asked.
  4. Answer in the language the question was asked in.
  5. Keep answers to 30–45 seconds. Stop talking.
  6. If you do not know, say so, then offer what you do know.
  7. Never criticise a previous employer, teacher or bank.
  8. Never say "I need this job because I need money", even if it is true.
  9. Say yes to relocation, and mean it.
  10. Close with "Thank you, sir/madam" and leave the chair straight.

H. The one thing they are really testing

A TCO handles other people's money all day, in a small room, often alone with a drawer of cash. The board is not looking for brilliance. It is looking for someone who will count twice, report honestly, and not take what is not theirs. Every answer above is built on that one sentence.
