DBBL's English section is rule-based, not literature-based. In the 2019 and 2017 papers the English MCQs were: fill-in-the-blank (grammar + vocabulary), odd word out, spelling, and sentence correction. These 25 rules cover the majority.

Rule 1 — Subject–verb agreement with "each / every / either / neither"

Singular: Each, every, either, neither, everyone, nobody, somebody take a singular verb. - Each of the students has a book. - Neither of the answers is correct.

Rule 2 — "The number of" vs "a number of"

  • The number of + plural noun → singular verb: The number of applicants is rising.
  • A number of + plural noun → plural verb: A number of students are absent.

Rule 3 — Neither…nor / either…or

The verb agrees with the nearer subject. - Neither the manager nor the employees were present. - Neither the employees nor the manager was present.

Rule 4 — Collective nouns

Words like team, committee, government, family take a singular verb when acting as one unit. - The committee has taken its decision.

Rule 5 — Uncountables

Advice, information, furniture, luggage, scenery, news, equipment, machinery are singular/uncountable. - The news is good. · He gave me some advice. (not "an advice")

Rule 6 — "Since" vs "for"

  • Since + point of time (2015, Monday, morning)
  • For + period of time (ten years, two hours)

Rule 7 — Preposition after adjectives

  • senior / junior / superior / inferior / prior to
  • married / engaged / addicted to
  • angry with a person, angry at/about a thing
  • good / bad / excellent at
  • capable / afraid / accused / deprived / full of (accused of, capable of)
  • insist / depend / rely / congratulate on
  • consist of, die of a disease, die from a cause

Rule 8 — Preposition after verbs

  • discuss (no "about"), enter (no "into" for a room), reach (no "to")
  • comprise / consist of — not "comprise of"
  • prefer X to Y (not "than")

Rule 9 — Second conditional

If + past simple, would + base verb — for unreal present. - If I were you, I would apologise.

Rule 10 — Third conditional

If + had + past participle, would have + past participle — for unreal past. - If he had studied, he would have passed.

Rule 11 — Inversion with "Had"

  • Had I known the truth, I would have come. (= If I had known)

Rule 12 — "As if / as though"

Takes the past subjunctive when the situation is unreal. - He speaks as if he knew everything.

Rule 13 — "It is high time"

Followed by past simple. - It is high time we started.

Rule 14 — "Would rather"

  • would rather + base verb: I would rather stay.
  • would rather … than + base verb: I would rather die than beg.

Rule 15 — Verbs followed by gerund (-ing)

mind, enjoy, avoid, deny, admit, suggest, finish, keep, stop, cannot help, look forward to, be used to, insist on, capable of, fond of - Would you mind opening the window? - He cannot help laughing. - She is used to getting up early.

Rule 16 — Verbs followed by infinitive

want, decide, hope, promise, refuse, agree, manage, fail, offer, expect - He refused to go.

Rule 17 — Relative pronouns

  • who → person (subject); whom → person (object); whose → possession
  • which → thing; that → person or thing (defining clauses)
  • The man whose car was stolen reported it.

Rule 18 — Comparatives

  • Use than with comparatives: taller than
  • Do not use "more" with -er adjectives: ~~more taller~~
  • Senior/junior/superior/inferior take to, not than.

Rule 19 — Double comparative / double negative

Avoid both. - ~~more better~~ → better - ~~didn't do nothing~~ → didn't do anything

Rule 20 — Articles

  • a before consonant sound: a European, a university, a one-taka note
  • an before vowel sound: an honest man, an hour, an MBA
  • The choice depends on sound, not spelling.

Rule 21 — Active → passive

Object becomes subject; verb becomes be + past participle; agent with by. - They built the bridge → The bridge was built. - Who broke the glass? → By whom was the glass broken?

Rule 22 — Direct → indirect speech

  • Present → past; "I" → "he/she"; "today" → "that day"; "tomorrow" → "the next day"
  • He said, "I am ill." → He said that he was ill.
  • He asked, "Where do you live?" → He asked where I lived.
  • Yes/no question → if/whether: He asked if I knew him.

Rule 23 — Sentence correction traps

  • different from (not different than)
  • one of the + plural noun: one of the books
  • a list of + plural may take a singular verb if "list" is the subject: A list of books is on the table.
  • discuss about → discuss
  • return back → return

Rule 24 — Spelling rules

  • i before e except after c: believe, receive
  • Double the consonant before -ing/-ed when the stress is on the last syllable: beginning, occurred, preferred
  • Common DBBL spellings: accommodation, occurrence, maintenance, committee, embarrass, privilege, separate, necessary, government, environment
