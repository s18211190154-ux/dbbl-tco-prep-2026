Written Part — How to Score Without Being a Great Writer

Practice

Warm up with 10 English MCQs

Then write one answer by hand against the clock.

The written part is not a literature test. It is a structure test. Examiners scan for a clear opening, organised points, and a conclusion. Give them that and your vocabulary barely matters.

1. What DBBL has actually asked

From the two real papers in 00-Past-Papers-Scanned/:

Year Written tasks
2019 (i) Problem solving — Venn diagram set problem; (ii) Problem solving — tree-planting geometry; (iii) Critical writing — consumer action vs government intervention; (iv) Essay (15 marks) — "Innovation and National Development"
2017 Written math — profit & loss, mixture, set theory, speed; descriptive writing

Pattern: 2 math problems with full steps + 1 argument/critical piece + 1 essay or paragraph. Total 40–50 marks.

2. Time budget (assume 60 minutes for the written part)

Task Marks Time
Math problem 1 10 10 min
Math problem 2 10 10 min
Paragraph / short note 10 10 min
Argument or essay 15–20 25 min
Checking — 5 min

Do the math first. It is objective, it is quick if you know the method, and it banks marks before your hand gets tired.

3. The paragraph formula (10 marks, 120–150 words)

Sentence 1  — Definition / what it is
Sentence 2  — Why it matters / context in Bangladesh
Sentence 3  — One benefit (with a small example)
Sentence 4  — One problem or limitation
Sentence 5  — What should be done
Sentence 6  — Closing line that echoes the first sentence

Do not write a list of bullet points. Do not exceed 180 words. Do not start with "Nowadays" — every weak answer starts that way. Start with the subject.

Strong openings: - "A cashless economy is one in which..." - "Financial inclusion means..." - "A cash officer is the first point of contact..."

4. The argument formula (15 marks, 200–250 words)

Para 1 (2–3 lines)  — Restate the issue + state your position clearly
Para 2 (4–5 lines)  — Your strongest reason + example
Para 3 (3–4 lines)  — The opposing view, and why it is weaker / limited
Para 4 (2–3 lines)  — Conclusion that repeats your position in different words

Position words: I largely agree / I partly disagree / While X has merit, I believe… Never sit on the fence. An examiner cannot award a position mark to "both sides are equal".

5. The essay formula (15–20 marks, 300–350 words)

Introduction   — define the topic, state the line you will take (3–4 lines)
Body 1         — first major point + example
Body 2         — second major point + example
Body 3         — a counterpoint or a Bangladesh-specific angle
Conclusion     — summarise and give a forward-looking sentence

Four paragraphs. One idea per paragraph. If you write one giant block, you lose presentation marks even if the content is good.

6. Written math — how to present steps

Examiners give method marks, not just the final number.

Given:        write down what is given, with units
To find:      write what is asked
Formula:      write the formula you will use
Working:      one step per line, no mental arithmetic
Answer:       box or underline the final answer with the unit

Even if the final answer is wrong, a clean method usually earns 60–70% of the marks. A correct answer with no working can earn almost nothing.

7. Formal email format (if asked)

To: [recipient]
Subject: [short, specific]

Dear Sir/Madam,

[Purpose in the first line.]
[Details: account number, date, amount, card number — whatever is relevant.]
[What you want them to do.]
[Polite closing line.]

Yours sincerely,
[Full name]
[Account number / contact number]

Keep it under 150 words. One purpose per email.

9. Ten topics likely to appear (prepare 5, not 10)

Banking / economy 1. Cashless Bangladesh / digital banking 2. Financial inclusion 3. The role of a cash officer 4. Inflation and the common people 5. Mobile financial services and rural development 6. Non-performing loans and the banking sector

General 7. Innovation and national development (asked in 2019) 8. Youth unemployment 9. Climate change and Bangladesh 10. The importance of discipline in professional life

10. Presentation rules that quietly earn marks

  1. Margin on both sides. Leave 2 cm.
  2. One idea per paragraph.
  3. Underline the topic sentence if you have time — it helps the examiner.
  4. Do not use more than one exclamation mark in the whole paper. None is better.
  5. Avoid SMS language — no "&", "u", "etc" in excess.
  6. Spell the bank's name correctly: Dutch-Bangla Bank PLC.
  7. Number your answers exactly as the question paper does.
  8. Cross out neatly with a single line. Do not scribble.
  9. Finish. An incomplete answer with a conclusion scores better than a perfect answer that stops mid-sentence.
  10. Leave 4–5 lines at the end of each answer so you can add a sentence if you remember something.

11. Common mistakes that cost marks

Mistake Fix
Writing 400 words for a 150-word paragraph Count sentences, not feelings. 6 sentences.
No conclusion Always write a last line that closes the answer
Copying the question as the first line Paraphrase it
Mixing "affect" and "effect" Affect = verb, Effect = noun
Writing "In my opinion, I think" Pick one
Illegible handwriting Slow down 10%; legibility beats speed
Leaving math answers without units Tk, kg, km, %, days — always
