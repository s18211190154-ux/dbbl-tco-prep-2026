## The 60-second strategy

1. **Math is the differentiator.** Most TCO candidates lose marks on percentage, profit-loss, ratio and time-work. You will not — you have 60 solved problems here.
2. **English grammar is predictable.** Sentence correction, fill-in-the-blank and spelling repeat across DBBL papers. Learn the 25 rules, not the whole grammar book.
3. **Banking GK is worth more than world GK** for a bank exam. Know repo/SLF/SDF, CRR/SLR, inflation, reserves, and the bank's own facts.
4. **ICT is free marks.** 15 shortcut keys and 6 definitions cover most of what DBBL asks.
5. **Don't leave blanks** unless there is negative marking and you are guessing blind. DBBL has not published a negative-marking rule for 2026.
6. **In the written part, structure beats length.** A 150-word answer with a clear intro-body-conclusion scores higher than 400 unstructured words.

## Using the mistake tracker

`mistake-tracker.csv` has 11 columns:

`Date · Subject · Topic · Source · Question No · My Answer · Correct Answer · Reason for Mistake · Rule or Formula to Revisit · Revisit Date · Status`

Rules for it:

1. **Log a mistake the same day.** A mistake you do not write down is a mistake you will repeat.
2. Write the **reason**, not "silly mistake". "Applied profit % on SP instead of CP" is useful; "careless" is not.
3. Set a revisit date 2 days later, then 7 days later. Change Status to `Fixed` only when you get it right twice.
4. Before the exam, read only the **Reason** column. That is your personal error profile.
