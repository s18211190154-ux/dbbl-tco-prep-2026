The confirmed facts

Bank Dutch-Bangla Bank PLC (DBBL)

Posting Anywhere in Bangladesh

Critical: MTO, AO and TCO sit the same paper slot on the same day. You compete only against other TCO applicants for TCO seats, but the paper may be a common/shared set with post-specific cut-offs. Do not assume an easy paper because the post is junior.

What the paper probably looks like

DBBL has not published a marks distribution. Based on the two real papers in 00-Past-Papers-Scanned/ (PO 2019: 100 marks / 90 min; PO 2017: 90 marks / 60 min) and published question banks, plan for this:

Part I — MCQ (likely 50–100 marks)

Section Likely weight What actually appears
English 20–25% Sentence correction, fill-in-the-blank (grammar + vocabulary), spelling, synonym/antonym, analogy, idioms
Mathematics 20–25% Percentage, profit & loss, ratio, average, time & work, speed, interest, number concept, age, mixture, sets, geometry, series
Analytical / IQ 15–20% Seating, grouping, blood relation, coding-decoding, direction, series, syllogism, data sufficiency, clock
General Knowledge 15–20% Bangladesh affairs, international affairs, banking & economy
ICT / Computer 5–10% MS Office shortcuts, hardware/software, networking, security
Bangla 0–20% Unconfirmed for TCO. Junior private-bank posts often include it — prepared defensively here

Part II — Written (likely 40–50 marks)

Historically DBBL's descriptive part contains: 1. Problem solving — 2 math questions, full steps required (Venn diagram, geometry, profit-loss, time-work, speed) 2. Critical writing / argument — agree/disagree on a statement 3. Essay or focused paragraph — 150–250 words 4. Occasionally precise writing or a formal email/application

Marking and negative marking

  • The 2026 circular and the admit-card instructions found do not mention negative marking.
  • DBBL's historical papers are simple 1-mark-per-question with no published penalty.
  • Practical rule: answer everything you can reason to 50/50 or better; leave a pure blind guess only if you see a penalty notice on the question paper itself. Read the instructions on page 1 before you start.

How the TCO paper differs from MTO

MTO TCO
Difficulty Highest Moderate
Written part Heavy — essay, critical analysis Lighter — paragraph, short argument, math
Emphasis Analytical + business awareness Accuracy, arithmetic, cash-handling common sense
Cut-off High Lower, but applicant pool is large
Interview Leadership/business Honesty, accuracy, customer service, willingness to relocate

Do not over-prepare for brilliance. Under-prepare for accuracy and you lose. TCO selection rewards people who make zero silly mistakes on easy arithmetic.

The 6 topics that decide TCO results

  1. Percentage / profit-loss / discount — appears every year, in MCQ and written
  2. Ratio, average, partnership — 4–6 MCQs typically
  3. Time & work / pipes — 2–3 MCQs plus one written problem
  4. Sentence correction + fill-in-the-blank — 10+ MCQs
  5. Banking GK (rates, ratios, regulators, digital payments) — 5–8 MCQs
  6. MS Office shortcut keys — 2–4 near-free marks

If you master only these six, you are already above the median candidate.

Exam-hall plan (memorise this)

Minutes 0–2: Fill every box on the OMR/answer sheet. Check the roll number twice.

Minutes 2–40: First pass — answer every question you can do in under 40 seconds. Skip and mark the rest. Do not get stuck.

Minutes 40–55: Second pass — the medium questions. Math with clean numbers first; long logic puzzles last.

Minutes 55–65: Third pass — educated guesses, then bubble everything. No blank rows.

Written part: Read all questions first. Do the math problem you are most confident about first (it anchors your score). Then the paragraph. Then the essay. Budget roughly 1 minute per mark.

Last 5 minutes: Check that question numbers on the answer sheet match the question paper. Off-by-one bubbling is the single most common avoidable disaster.
